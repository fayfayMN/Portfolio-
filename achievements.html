MinneMUDAC 2026 — Graduate Division

Oct 17, 2026 · St. Catherine University

Competed in the Graduate Division as an undergraduate, architecting an end-to-end Python pipeline joining 8 operational and public datasets (Census ACS, USDA Food Access Atlas, BLS, SNAP) across 87 Minnesota counties and 55 months.

Ranked #6 in Graduate Division Prediction Accuracy; evaluated 8 candidate models via walk-forward backtesting and applied damped smoothing to out-forecast complex SARIMA architectures.

The Finding That Almost Wasn’t

The first cut said Minnesota’s food shelf network reaches the neighborhoods USDA flags as low-access. We almost stopped there. But the comparison group included wealthy suburbs that would never host a shelf. We held income constant and re-ran it — the advantage vanished (Fisher’s exact p = 0.76). Under USDA’s strictest distance definition, the result reversed.

The network tracks neighborhood poverty, not grocery access. The Food Group requested the team’s slide deck for operational siting strategy.

Also quantified SNAP benefit-cliff policy shocks using an interrupted time series with Newey–West HAC robust standard errors (+22% visits, p < 0.001).

The lesson: Almost-wrong → caught it → corrected. The first answer looked clean, but the comparison group was flawed. Checking my own work turned a misleading confirmation into the real finding.
