What the data said

The censoring trap

As stockout hours rise, observed sales fall (1.14 → 0.46) but stockout-adjusted demand rises (1.14 → 4.72) — most of the decline is censoring, not lost demand. We built a transparent est_demand proxy and flagged it as an MNAR (non-ignorable) pattern.

What actually moves sales

Discount (r ≈ −0.21) and holidays (r ≈ +0.17) matter most; weekends run ~50% above weekdays; weather is near zero. All VIF < 3 — no multicollinearity.

The verdict

Scored once on the dataset's official future split, every model beats the naive baseline — advance information gives a modest but real lift over past sales alone. The simple linear model held up best on RMSE; the forest's within-sample edge did not carry into the true future.

Limitations stated up front: we predict observed sales, not full unmet demand; a 90-day window can't establish annual seasonality; the 100-series convenience sample limits generalization to the full chain. My work spans notebooks 01–05.
